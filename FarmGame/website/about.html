ABOUT THE GAME

The story, the characters, and the farm behind the madness.

THE STORY

BORRIS

Deep in the pixel countryside sits a small but dangerous farm. Borris the Bunny has one job to save his family from the farmer and his animals

Armed with quick legs, a dodge roll, and a surprisingly solid right hook, Borris must outmanoeuvre the enemies, snatch every last carrot, and make it out alive. Fail, and the Game Over screen reminds you exactly how many carrots you managed to grab before the farmer won.

Built with Python 3 and Pygame, the game features sprite-sheet animation, enemy AI with separation steering, collision-based combat, a dodge mechanic with cooldown, and a persistent health system.

CHARACTERS

BORRIS THE BUNNY

The player character. 8-frame animated sprite, moves at speed 5, dodge speed 18. 100 HP. Attacks for 15 damage with a 55-pixel range. Invincible during dodge frames.

THE FOXES

Three hunters. 4-frame animation, speed 3. Chase Borris using direction vectors + separation steering. 100 HP each. Deal 5 damage on contact. Flash white when struck.

SCARECROW

A permanent fixture of the farm. Doesn't move, doesn't help — but stands as a pixel-art landmark on the level, giving the farm that authentic countryside feel.

THE CARROTS

Seven collectibles placed across the level, each surrounded by a soft golden glow. Touch one to add it to your tally. Collect all 7 to win the round.

TECH STACK

Py

PYTHON 3 & PYGAME

The entire game runs on Python 3 with the Pygame library handling the display surface, sprite groups, image loading, collision detection, and the 60 FPS game loop. No external engine — just pure Python.

🎨

HAND-CRAFTED PIXEL ART

All art assets are pixel sprites: a rabbit sprite sheet with 8 walk frames across a 3×3 grid at 64×64 px, a 4-frame fox sheet at 340×340 px per cell, and tiled background levels with a house, scarecrow, and farm scenery.

🤖

ENEMY AI

Foxes use a lightweight steering system: each frame they compute a direction vector toward Borris, then add a separation force that pushes them away from nearby foxes. This keeps them spread out and avoids clumping, making them feel more alive without pathfinding.
